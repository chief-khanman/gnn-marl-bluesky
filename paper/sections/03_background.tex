\section{Background and Related Work}
\subsection{Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning}
CTDE methods such as QMIX~\cite{qmix} and MAPPO~\cite{mappo} train decentralized policies
with a centralized component used only during learning. The QMIX mixing network and the
MAPPO centralized critic take a joint input whose dimensionality is fixed by the training
team size, so changing $n$ requires architectural modification and retraining. This
hard coupling between agent count and network architecture is the primary barrier to
scalable AAM autonomy that the present work addresses.

\subsection{Graph Neural Networks for Multi-Agent Systems}
Graph-based MARL~\cite{gnn_marl} represents the fleet as a graph and exploits the
permutation equivariance of message passing to produce representations that do not depend
on agent ordering. The present work extends this principle by positioning the GNN as an
encoder whose global readout produces a latent vector of fixed dimension $d$, rather than
using the GNN as a replacement for the mixer (as in GraphMIX) or as an observation
enrichment tool (as in QMIX-GNN and InforMARL). This placement fully decouples the
downstream Q-network from $n$ and is compatible with any CTDE value-based method.

\subsection{Safety-Critical and Scale-Invariant Control}
Neural Graph Control Barrier Functions~\cite{gcbf} provide distributed collision avoidance
with a formal safety certificate by encoding inter-agent safety constraints directly into
a graph-structured control law. In the present framework, GCBF serves a dual role: as a
baseline for empirical comparison and as a principled mechanism for determining the
intervention threshold $\theta$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:switching}. Specifically, the CBF
condition $\dot{h}^{ij} + \alpha(h^{ij}) \geq 0$, where $h^{ij}$ is the barrier function
between vehicles $i$ and $j$ and $\alpha$ is a class-$\mathcal{K}$ function, provides a
computable, certificate-backed criterion for when the PID controller alone is insufficient
to guarantee separation. The learned safety score $s^i$ is trained to approximate this
barrier condition, and $\theta$ is calibrated such that $s^i < \theta$ coincides with the
barrier condition being at risk of violation. This grounds the switching logic of the
hybrid controller in a formally motivated safety criterion rather than an ad hoc
heuristic, and provides a path toward certificate-based guarantees for the hybrid
control architecture as future work.

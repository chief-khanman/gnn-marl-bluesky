\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
\subsection{Cooperative Conflict Resolution}
\label{subsec:problem}
We consider $n$ cooperating vehicles that fly at a common altitude in a shared airspace
sector. Each vehicle $i$ is assigned its own waypoint and is controlled through a heading
command and a speed command. Two vehicles $i$ and $j$ are in loss of separation (LoS) at a
control step when their horizontal distance satisfies $d_{ij} < d_{\text{sep}}$, with
$d_{\text{sep}} = 5$~NM. Each vehicle must fly toward its waypoint while the fleet avoids
losses of separation.

The problem is a Dec-POMDP as defined in Section~\ref{subsec:decpomdp}. The state $s$
contains the position, velocity, and waypoint-tracking error of every vehicle. Vehicle $i$
observes its own tracking error and airspeed and the relative position and velocity of the
vehicles inside its sensor radius $r_{\text{detect}}$. The action $a_i$ is one of 15
discrete combinations of a heading change and a speed change
(Section~\ref{subsec:env}). Each vehicle receives an individual reward $r_i$ that penalizes
deviation from the bearing to its waypoint and penalizes every other vehicle closer than
$d_{\text{sep}}$; the team reward used for learning is the mean over the fleet,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:team-reward}
r = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} r_i .
\end{equation}
The mean is used instead of the sum so that the scale of the return does not grow with the
fleet size. The number of vehicles is constant within an episode.

\subsection{Generalization Across Fleet Size}
\label{subsec:generalization}
Let $\mathcal{N}_{\text{train}}$ and $\mathcal{N}_{\text{test}}$ be two disjoint sets of fleet
sizes. The goal is a single set of parameters $\theta$, trained only on episodes with
$n\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{train}}$, that defines the individual action-value functions $Q_i$
and can be executed without any change or further training on episodes with
$n\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{test}}$. We call this zero-shot transfer across fleet size. In this
paper $\mathcal{N}_{\text{train}}=\{3,6,9\}$ and $\mathcal{N}_{\text{test}}=\{10,20,50\}$.
Zero-shot transfer requires that (a) the agent network accepts observations that describe
any number of other vehicles, and (b) for joint training over $\mathcal{N}_{\text{train}}$,
the centralized mixing function accepts any number of agents. Standard value-decomposition
networks meet neither requirement (Section~\ref{subsec:monotonic}).

\subsection{Communication}
\label{subsec:communication}
Execution uses no communication between vehicles: each vehicle acts on what its own sensor
measures inside $r_{\text{detect}}$.
